Embodied RSI: Better Tasks, Better Improvers?

Research review · English / 中文
On this page

The key to recursive self-improvement is not how many times a loop runs, but what the next iteration inherits—and what that inheritance achieves.

This review separates the task system from the process that improves it, examines four embodied AI papers, and uses Ai2’s evaluation study and AIDE²’s ignition test to assess the evidence.

THE CENTRAL DISTINCTION

Task gains, persistent updates and improved improvement capability are different evidence claims.

The embodied cases reviewed here mainly support L2. PhysEvo offers L3 structural evidence; controlled evidence of L3 gains remains a separate question.

1 · Concepts and evidence#

Two functional roles#

The task system, SSS, performs tasks. It includes the model, tools, skills, memory and code. The improvement process, III, diagnoses failures, proposes changes, searches over candidates and arranges validation. An improver implements that process; it need not be a separate model. One model can serve both roles. 1

Updating and retaining the task system is self-improvement in a broad sense. Recursion further concerns changes to the method that produces future improvements. A recursive structure does not by itself establish greater improvement capability.

What L1, L2 and L3 establish#

LevelObject of the claimEvidence required
L1: task or artifact gainA current answer, program artifact or execution resultA better result, without established persistent change to the task system
L2: persistent task-system updateModel weights, skills, memory or execution codeChanges are retained and used in subsequent execution; gains and transfer are reported separately
L3 structural evidenceThe process producing future improvementsA modified diagnosis, search or update mechanism is inherited by later improvement rounds
L3 gain evidenceThe effectiveness of the improvement processFrom the same starting system, with comparable budgets and evaluation, the new process produces better successors

These are the report’s operational distinctions. “Strong L2” describes substantial experimental support for retention and task gains; it is not another formal level. Persistence and generalization are separate properties. Limited transfer does not imply that no system update occurred.

Key terms#

TermMeaning here
AgentA complete system that observes, decides and acts; not just a model

2 · Six mechanisms and a shared loop#

Wu et al.’s survey organizes work by update mechanism. The six mechanisms describe what changes and how; L1/L2/L3 describe what the evidence establishes. Neither replaces the other. 1

MechanismWhat changes
WeightsModel parameters updated through training
TasksWhat the next round practices, explores or collects
MemoryExperience stored and retrieved for later decisions
ProgramsTools, skills, prompts or workflow code
FeedbackSignals or mechanisms used to assess outcomes
ImproverThe method for diagnosis, search and generating the next update
01 · ExecuteProduce task experience
02 · DiagnoseLocate failures and weaknesses
03 · ModifyPropose candidate updates
04 · ValidateCheck gains, regressions and cost
05 · RetainReuse accepted changes

Software and robots share this loop. Robotics additionally incurs physical interaction, scene-reset, hardware-wear and human-intervention costs. Feedback mechanisms may improve, but final evaluation standards must remain protected: changing the scoring rule must not masquerade as better performance.

3 · Two update targets and a research workflow#

Physical RSI is a convergence of mechanisms rather than one standardized algorithm. After observing and diagnosing failures, a system can repair its harness or skills, collect targeted experience, update policy weights, and validate and retain the result. These branches can be combined.

  • Harness and skill updates: experience changes tools, code, memory and reusable skills; model weights may remain frozen. PhysEvo and RoboRSI are the central cases here.
  • Model and policy updates: failures change the next training data, data changes the policy, and the new policy changes the failure distribution. EmbodiRSI is the central case.
  • Automated research: describes who proposes solutions, runs experiments and selects results. ENPIRE can edit programs or organize policy training, overlapping both update targets. It is not a third parallel kind of object being updated.

4 · Four embodied papers#

PhysEvo: updating execution tools and diagnostic resources#

Mechanism: model weights stay frozen while execution feedback drives tool and skill changes. The Task Harness supports execution; the Meta Harness supports diagnosis and improvement. Tools used to diagnose future failures can themselves be rewritten and retained.

Retained: reusable execution capabilities and diagnostic resources.

Evidence: strong L2 + L3 structure. An independent comparison of old and new improvers, starting from the same task system under comparable total budgets, is still needed to isolate improvement capability. 2

RoboRSI: accumulating reusable skill repairs#

Organization: people provide goals, constraints and corrections; the system maintains a task–skill hierarchy. The Manager manages and reviews, the Planner plans, the Engineer implements and invokes skills, and the Reviewer checks and diagnoses. These are roles, not necessarily four separately trained models.

TSR—Top-Down Skill Refinement: locate the failed node → modify the responsible branch → check new tasks and existing capabilities → release a reusable version.

Retained: skill code, interfaces, checks and composite skills.

Evidence: strong L2. The paper reports 104 rounds of real-robot development, alongside simulation reuse and transfer experiments. It does not establish L3 gains. An additional ACT experiment—training a policy that predicts a chunk of actions—does not mean the main loop retrains a general model at every iteration. 3

EmbodiRSI: changing data, then changing the policy#

Loop: execute the policy → identify failures → correct errors or collect targeted data → train a new policy → execute again.

Six modules: Real2Sim reconstructs simulation; Trajectory Generation creates trajectories; Policy Training & Rollout trains and executes; Collaborative Error Correction generates corrective trajectories; Adaptive Data Collection targets weaknesses; Sim2Real adapts the policy using limited real experience.

Training versus testing: during training, GPT-5.5 agents organize data and correction, planning tools generate trajectories, and training code updates the robot policy; real-world refinement includes human participation. Formal testing disables external takeovers, planner recovery and human correction, leaving the trained policy to execute independently.

Result: across 3 scenes and 14 subtasks, 400 simulation trajectories plus 10 real refinement trajectories per subtask yield 83.1% real-world scene-balanced success, versus 75.0% for a baseline using 200 real demonstrations. Scene-balanced scoring weights scenes equally. The retained real training trajectories are 1/20 of the baseline count; total cost is not thereby 1/20.

Evidence: strong L2. Data and policy weights persist. Policy improvement does not demonstrate that the data-collection or training method has itself improved; the coordinating language model is not trained by this loop. 4

ENPIRE: organizing real-robot research automatically#

Organization: a research agent proposes solutions, edits code, runs real experiments and selects candidates using fixed evaluation feedback.

Updates: policy programs, training-algorithm code, trained weights and research experience. Reset, success-verification and safety interfaces remain fixed after environment construction.

Relationship to the other cases: both program editing and policy training can be research choices. Automated research describes the workflow, not a third update target alongside code and weights.

Evidence: L2 + indications of inherited improvement resources. Retained and transferred research experience deserves separate analysis, but automated experimentation alone cannot establish L3 gains. 5

5 · Comparing retained changes and evidence#

WorkMain retained changesAssessment in this review
PhysEvoExecution tools, skills and diagnostic resourcesStrong L2 + L3 structure; improver gains require separate testing
RoboRSISkill code, interfaces and checksStrong L2; task gains are not improver gains
EmbodiRSIDatasets and policy weightsStrong L2; higher success does not establish self-upgrading collection or training methods
ENPIREPolicy/training code, weights and research experienceL2 + inheritance indications; audit policies and research processes separately

Comparisons should report update targets, retained changes, budgets, real versus simulated environments, held-out evaluation and limitations. Success rates from different environments, data budgets and metrics do not form a directly comparable leaderboard.

6 · A software comparison: AIDE²#

For AIDE², this review defines the inner research agent as task system SSS and the outer rewriting and selection process as III. Improving the inner agent does not automatically improve the outer process.

The main experiment ran for 8 days, produced an evolutionary trace of 100 nodes, accepted 7 rewrites, and raised the private score from 0.703 to 0.778, with additional external benchmark evaluation. Because private scores inform candidate selection, they are not equivalent to a final test untouched during development. The paper separately introduces an ignition test asking whether the evolved agent is better at serving as the outer improver. 6

7 · Ai2: evolution gains or more search budget?#

Ai2 is the Allen Institute for AI. Its evaluation paper compares parallel sampling, sequential revision, cross-task harness evolution and task-specific harness adaptation, with comparable feedback and inference budgets. 7

Terminal-Bench settingParallel samplingHarness evolution
No unit tests, mean pass@172.3%67.4%
With unit tests, mean pass@182.0%69.7%

Terminal-Bench evaluates terminal-operation tasks. Pass@1 measures the passing performance of a single output, although the overall method can use feedback to select candidates. The experiments use three models, two independent runs, and sampling/iteration budget parameter K=5K=5K=5.

The held-out experiment uses 45 training, 10 validation and 34 test tasks. Mean pass@1 across three models is 63.2% → 63.2%, showing no average improvement.

The conclusion is not that harness evolution never works. Version 4 also reports a positive case in long-horizon interactive games, emphasizing how tasks, feedback detail and budgets affect outcomes. Robotics should likewise account separately for model calls, interactions, resets, training and human intervention. This is the present review’s implication for robotics, not a robot experiment performed by Ai2.

8 · The ignition test: has the improver improved?#

Fix the starting system, budget and evaluation#

What AIDE² actually compared#

Both groups begin from the same inner agent, AIDE₄₇. One uses the evolved AIDE₄₇ as the outer improver; the other uses the human-engineered AIDE_human. The latter is an agent system, not a person performing the experiments live. Each group uses 3 random seeds and 50 steps per seed. 6

The authors’ conclusion is inconclusive. Signs that the evolved improver reaches near-final quality earlier do not establish a reliable advantage with only three seeds. An ignition test asks which process is better at generating the next improvement; it does not require AI to outperform humans.

9 · Returning to the survey’s questions#

  1. What changed? The current artifact, the task system, or the process producing modifications.
  2. What was retained? Whether updates participate in later tasks or improvement rounds.
  3. What was demonstrated? Task gains, transferable gains and improver gains each require evidence.

The cited embodied cases demonstrate persistent system updates; PhysEvo additionally provides structural evidence concerning the improvement process. Higher robot success alone does not show that the improver is better. This conclusion concerns the versions and experiments reviewed here, not a claim that such evidence cannot exist anywhere in embodied AI.
