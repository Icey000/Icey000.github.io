Part 01 / Lecture 1

Affordances: what can I do?#

Task: Document one good and one bad affordance with a picture or video. Explain both and propose a fix for the bad example.

An affordance is an action an object allows, such as pressing or grasping. A signifier helps a person discover that action. A misleading handle can allow grasping while suggesting the wrong way to open a door.

Case 1 / 6

Good example: iPhone volume buttons#

Goal. Adjust the sound while listening to music or watching a video.

Analysis. The raised side buttons provide a surface that can be pressed and located by touch. Their physical shape signals an available action. During playback, pressing them changes the sound level, allowing the user to check the effect immediately.

Pressability is the physical affordance; the protruding shape is a signifier. The relationship between the two buttons and increasing or decreasing volume is mapping, while the audible change is feedback. These concepts work together, but are not interchangeable.

The shape alone does not explain every context-dependent function. This positive example concerns media-volume adjustment, not every iPhone interaction.

Personal evidence to add: a photograph of the phone actually used, the app and task, and what happened when each button was pressed.

Case 2 / 6

Bad example: a pullable handle on a push door#

Goal. Open the door.

Analysis. The handle can be grasped and pulled, yet the word PUSH specifies the required direction. The photographer reports that the design confused her and describes another visitor trying to pull a similarly equipped door. That is the source author’s observation, not my own.

The handle’s physical affordance and visual suggestion compete with the intended action. A user may pull first, receive no opening movement, and incorrectly conclude that the door is locked.

Redesign. Replace the handle on the push side with a flat push plate.

Proposed redesign · schematic
PUSH

Use a flat push plate on this side.
Retain a pull handle on the side that requires pulling.

A plate offers a place to press without an inviting gap to grip. The physical cue and the door’s motion agree. This proposal has not been tested.

Personal evidence to add: a real door you encounter, its required opening direction, and a photo or short video of the interaction.

Part 02 / Lecture 2

Gestalt laws: what belongs together?#

Task: Document two daily-life applications of Gestalt laws that went wrong, including digital examples, and suggest corrections.

The course introduces six principles: proximity, similarity, common region, continuity, closure and figure–ground. This task asks for two failures, not six separate definitions.

Case 3 / 6

Common region: an escalator warning splits the message#

Goal. Read the safety message before stepping onto the escalator.

Analysis. The intended sentence is KEEP OFF RED LINE. However, the border divides the words into two regions: KEEP / RED and OFF / LINE. Reading within each panel produces the wrong word groups.

Common region makes words inside the same boundary appear related. Here the visual grouping conflicts with the sentence’s intended reading order.

Redesign. Put the complete instruction inside one clearly defined region.

Proposed redesign · schematic
KEEP OFF
THE RED LINE

One message, one region. Keep the entire instruction together rather than splitting it across side-by-side mats.

The layout supports reading the instruction in order. Placement and legibility would still need checking from the approach direction.

This is a classroom reference. Replace or supplement it with a comparable sign you actually encounter; record where it is and how the grouping affects reading.

Case 4 / 6

Proximity: “Skip” is separated from the sign-in choices#

Goal. Continue into a travel app without creating an account.

Analysis. Sign-in choices sit together near the bottom, while Skip sits alone at the top. A person concentrating on the lower controls may miss the alternative.

Proximity suggests relatedness. Separating Skip from the other ways to continue makes the available choices harder to compare.

Redesign. Group all ways to continue together, including an explicitly labelled guest option.

Proposed redesign · schematic, not a live login
How would you like to continue?Sign in with AppleOther sign-in optionsContinue without an account

Putting the alternatives together and replacing Skip with an explicit label makes guest access easier to notice. This is a layout critique, not evidence that the separation was intentional.

Personal evidence to add: an app you actually use with separated related controls, its version or capture date, and an annotated screenshot.

Part 03 / Lecture 3

Dark patterns: whose goal comes first?#

Task: Document two dark design patterns and discuss how to redesign them to achieve the opposite effect: clear information and meaningful user choice.

Case 5 / 6

Disguised advertising: two competing download buttons#

Goal. Download VLC, the program named at the top of the page.

Analysis. A large green DOWNLOAD control appears inside an advertisement for another product. Its generic wording competes with the blue software-download control, so clicking the most noticeable button may lead away from the intended download.

Redesign. Make the destination clear before the click.

Proposed redesign · schematic
VLC media playerDownload VLC for Windows
ADVERTISEMENT · DIFFERENT PRODUCT

Privacy Keeper browser extension

Learn about this extension →

Name the downloaded product and platform. Separate advertising visually, use an obvious ad label, and avoid a second ambiguous Download button. The intended result is fewer mistaken clicks, not simply a different button colour.

Case 6 / 6

Obstruction: accepting is easier than refusing#

Goal. Continue using the service while deciding whether partner data may be used for advertising.

Analysis. Accept and Continue offers an immediate route. Refusing requires entering Manage Data Settings, changing a toggle and saving. The extra effort makes one choice easier to finish than the other.

Redesign. Support an informed decision with comparable effort for acceptance and refusal.

Proposed redesign · schematic
Use data from partners for advertising?

Choose whether information from other sites and apps can be used for this purpose.

Allow and continueDo not allow and continue
Read details

Present both choices at the same level, with equal visual weight and one clear action each. After either choice, confirm the saved setting and explain how to change it later.
